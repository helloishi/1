      About PhoneGuard

      Why it exists

      71,391

      mobile phone thefts and robberies recorded by the Met in London in 2025, down 12.3% from 81,365 in 2024, but up 54% since 2022. Mayor of London, Feb 2026.

      £4.5m

      extra funding for a new Mobile Phone Theft Command Cell, set up by the Mayor (Feb 2026, same source).

      about 2.4%

      Of 587,498 phones reported stolen in London from 2017 to Feb 2024, only 13,998 were reported recovered. Reported by the GLA and the Met. Medium confidence.

      about 40%

      of thefts reported to happen in Westminster and the West End, with around 37 victims a day in the West End. Met figures as reported in the press. Medium confidence.

      PhoneGuard helps in two ways: settings and habits that make theft less rewarding, and a calm plan to block the SIM and phone quickly. Faster blocking cuts resale value and account takeover, and gives police fuller reports.

      How the data works

      The hotspot map uses street-level crime data from police.uk. Your phone asks police.uk directly for the latest published month (usually about two months behind). Reports are grouped by their approximate location. Police.uk moves each report to a nearby anonymous point, so places are not exact. The map shows reports of theft from the person and robbery. Use it to stay alert, not to avoid places.

      Privacy

      • No accounts, no analytics, no cookies, no trackers.
      • Your location stays on your phone. It is only used to centre the map and for Heads-up mode. Police.uk receives the map centre point (rounded) to return nearby reports, but not who you are.
      • Your ticks, settings and language are saved only in this browser.

      Please note

      This is a prototype. Menu names differ between phone makers and software versions, so look for similar wording. Check each network, bank and police service for the latest advice. If you are in danger, call 999.
